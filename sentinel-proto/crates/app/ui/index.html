    Help keep Sentinel running?

    Sentinel has no company servers. It runs on Pillars hosted by people like you. You can run one from this app while it's open.

    Read this before turning it on
    • Your Pillar keeps other people's public posts and sealed messages (which nobody can read, not even you) and passes them along over Tor.
    • Your IP address and location stay hidden: Pillars can only be reached through Tor.
    • Someone watching very closely could notice your Pillar goes offline when you do.

      Help run SentinelSentinel has no company servers. Everything runs on computers lent by people. All of these are optional, off unless you turn them on, and off in High-risk mode.
      

          
      
            
      
              
      
                Pillar
                Carries the network: keeps public posts and sealed messages for others and passes them along, while the app is open. Long-running Pillars kept by established operators also issue credits.
              
      
              
            
      
            
      
            
      
              The risks of running a Pillar
              
        
                
      • It stores other people's public posts and sealed messages on your computer (up to 2 GB, deleted after 60 days). Sealed messages can't be read, not even by you.
        • 
                
      • It's reachable only through Tor, so your IP address and location stay hidden.
        • 
                
      • Someone watching very closely could notice that your Pillar goes offline when you do, and use that to guess it's yours.
        • 
                
      • If your computer is taken, it will show that you ran a Pillar.
        • 
                
      • Only do this where it's legal and safe to use Tor and to keep other people's content.
        • 
              
      
            
      
          
      

          
      
            
      
              
      
                Archive
                Lends disk space for people's photos, videos and files, and earns credits every day. Runs inside your Pillar, so turning it on turns your Pillar on too.
              
      
              
            
      
            
            
      
            
      
              The risks of running an Archive
              
        
                
      • It keeps encrypted pieces of other people's files. Nobody can read them, including you and anyone who takes your computer, but that also means you can't check what they are.
        • 
                
      • In some places, keeping data you can't inspect is itself risky. Only do this where it's legal and safe.
        • 
                
      • Archives are contacted more often than plain Pillars and checked at random times, so their online hours are easier to watch. Your IP address still stays hidden behind Tor.
        • 
                
      • It uses the disk space you choose and some internet bandwidth. Files are spread over several Archives, so nobody loses anything if yours goes away.
        • 
                
      • If your computer is taken, it will show that you ran an Archive.
        • 
              
      
            
      
            
      
              
      Also keep my own uploadsFaster and longer-lasting for your own files, but anyone who sees your posts learns your Archive's address and can watch when it's online. Off in High-risk mode.

            Release keys (for people who sign updates)
            
      
              Updates only install when most release key holders have signed them, so no single person (or stolen key) can push one. If you've been asked to hold a release key, do it here. Keep the key on a USB stick that stays unplugged, never on this computer.
              
      
                1. Become a key holder
                Plug in a USB stick, press the button and save the key there. Then send the line that appears to whoever builds releases. It's public: it can only check signatures, not make them.
                
      
                
      
                  
                  
      
                
      
              
      
              
      
                2. Sign an update you were sent
                Choose the update file. You'll see what's inside before signing. Only sign if you expected this update (ask the person who sent it if unsure).

            Panic wipe
            Erases this account, its keys, messages and Tor state from this device and quits. It can't be undone without a backup.
            
          
            
          
          
          
        
          
        
          
          
          
            Disguise as a calculator
            While Sentinel is locked, it looks and works like a calculator, named "Calculator" with a calculator icon (in the Start menu and on the desktop too). To unlock, type your code and press =.
            Honest limits: Task Manager and Windows' list of installed apps still say The Sentinel Protocol, and anyone who knows about this disguise knows to try it. It hides Sentinel from a glance, not from a search. If you pinned Sentinel to the taskbar, unpin it.
          
          
          
          
          
          
          
        
          
        
          
          
          
            Updates
            Sentinel isn't in any app store. Updates come from Pillars over Tor, or as a file someone hands you. Either way, the app only installs an update signed by a majority of Sentinel's release keys (with signatures a quantum computer can't forge) and newer than what you have. A changed byte anywhere and it's refused. Updates from Pillars must be carried by at least two of them, identically (so everyone gets the same one), and wait three days before they can be installed, so a bad release can be caught and revoked first.
          
          
          
          
          
          On phones, a new version comes as an app file (APK) from whoever gave you Sentinel. Android checks it's signed with the same key as the version you have before it installs.
